# Lo Floc: urban evolution of Riudoms (summary in my own words)

## Main article

**Citation:** Eugeni Perea Simón, "L'evolució urbanística de Riudoms [s. XII - XXI]". *Lo Floc*, no. 180 (2007), pp. 4-11. Centre d'Estudis Riudomencs Arnau de Palomar (CERAP).
- Article page: https://raco.cat/index.php/LoFloc/article/view/312106
- PDF: https://raco.cat/index.php/LoFloc/article/download/312106/402194

It is not published by the Associació Cultural Amics de l'Om, which publishes the monthly *L'Om*. Sources: the RACO journal page, and https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riudoms (section "Premsa local").

### Key points (paraphrased)

1. **Origin and medieval core (12th century onwards)**
   - Riudoms grew as a medieval settlement after the Christian resettlement. The first houses clustered under the protection of the castle.
   - The castle stood where the **Plaça de l'Església** is today.
   - The ground allowed the first streets to run in fairly straight, parallel lines: **Sant Jaume, d'Amunt, Nou, Major and d'Avall**.
   - These streets ran between the **eastern walls** (called "de la Font Nova" and "de l'Arenal") and the **western walls** (called "Vella" and "de les Galanes").
   - Present-day OSM streets still carry these names: *Carrer de la Muralla de la Font Nova*, *Carrer de la Muralla Vella*, *Carrer de l'Arenal* and *Carrer de les Galanes*. They trace the old wall lines.
2. **Life inside the walls**
   - The enclosure held houses with vegetable plots and yards, private or shared wells and ponds, flour mills, presses, brandy stills, and workshops: ovens, carpenters, locksmiths, tinsmiths.
   - Much of the work happened in the street.
   - As the population grew, the yards and plots were filled with houses and public buildings. What had first looked "more like a camp than a village" slowly became a dense urban core.
3. **Roman antecedent:** the site was occupied before the medieval village. Roman occupation inside today's village is attested at least from the 1st to the 6th century AD. The villa of la Mola is an example.
4. **Early Modern period (16th-18th centuries)**
   - Fortification stayed vital, and towers were built to watch for coastal raids.
   - The urban shape barely changed. The one notable exception was a small growth **outside the walls: the Raval de Sant Francesc**. It formed along the access road that entered the village by the **Portal de Reus**.
   - The raval housed farmers and herders with their sheep pens, and probably also a Jewish presence. No call or synagogue is documented.
   - From the late Middle Ages to the contemporary period the village stayed closed within **walls, gates (portals) and watch towers**.
5. **End of the walls (19th century)**
   - The Carlist wars were the last phase in which the walls had a role. By the Spanish Civil War they had none.
   - Hygiene reforms changed the old core:
     - small alleys ("corralons") were opened to air it;
     - underground sewers were built, replacing open drains;
     - a public **slaughterhouse (1899)** was built;
     - burials inside and around the church ended, and a new **cemetery (1865)** opened.
   - In the same period the **walls and gates were demolished or opened up**, and the watch towers disappeared. Some later maps still drew them.
   - A separate source adds that in 1873 Carlists burned the gates and pulled down part of the wall (https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riudoms).
6. **Photos of the gates (1939, L. Roisin, Arxiu Històric Municipal)**
   - the **Portal de l'Orient**, at the start of today's Avinguda de Pau Casals;
   - the **Portal de Ponent**, today's Plaça del Portal.
   - IPAC card 9668 adds that the **old church** probably stood at the **Portal de Torrent**.
7. **Second growth outside the walls (second half of the 19th century)**
   - **Barri del Colomer** was promoted by mayor and master builder Antoni Ortiga Caparó.
   - It was planned, with five streets (Sant Josep, Sant Pere, Sant Joan, Sant Tomàs and Sant Pau), uniform houses and calculated street widths. Carrer de Sant Jaume and Carrer de Sant Antoni linked it to the old core.
   - In the same years the **east side of the Plaça de l'Església was built up**: the *Porxos*, the block between the Arenal, Sant Isidre and the square.
   - The first modern public fountains (late 19th century) and the public washhouses on Carrer de Sant Antoni (1904) date from this phase.
8. **20th century**
   - **Barriada Lluís Massó ("Cases Barates")**
     - Designed in 1944 on the Hort del Camp.
     - The plan was for 196 homes and about 1,100 people.
     - Only a first phase was built, and building resumed in a disorderly way in the 1960s.
   - **Les Casetes** came after the war.
   - The unplanned **Parcel·les Ferran** (1960s) were built without basic services.
   - **Molí d'en Marc** (from the 1970s) is mostly chalets and terraced houses.
   - **La Soleiada** was subdivided around the 1980s.
   - **La Mola** was planned in 1996 with mixed building types.
   - **Mas del Llibori** lies between the Ermita de Sant Antoni and the cemetery.
9. **Squares, monuments and fountains**
   - Most squares are older than the 19th century. Perea regrets that 20th-century growth added almost no real squares.
   - Religious ornament dominated. Stone crosses stood in the Plaça de l'Arbre and at Sant Antoni (the "Pedró"), and both were destroyed in 1936.
   - The fountain of the Plaça de l'Om once had a religious image.
   - A Francoist war memorial stood in the **Plaça de l'Om from 1954**. It was later moved to the cemetery ([1998] in the article; other sources say 1997).
   - Ornamental fountains are dated: **Plaça Petita / "del Llac" (1889)** and **Plaça de l'Església (1976-77)**, with jets and colour effects.
   - A **monument to Josep Cros** made of upright rough stones was placed in the Plaça de l'Església in 2005.
   - Ramps were added to give access to the parish church.
10. **Built heritage listed in the article**
    - The parish church (16th-19th century) and the **Abadia** preside over the Plaça de l'Església, "on the site of the old castle".
    - Inside the old enclosure:
      - Verge Maria chapel and the Hospital (16th century);
      - Casa de la Vila and Cal Marc Massó (18th century);
      - les Monges, Sant Joan, the Casa del Beat Bonaventura, the slaughterhouse and the market (19th century).
    - Outside the old enclosure:
      - "convent de Sant Francesc" (16th century; the Franciscan convent, called Sant Joan in IPAC 9677);
      - Ermita de Sant Antoni (17th-18th century);
      - La Soleiada. The article says early 19th century, but IPAC says early 20th century, which is more likely.
11. **Building norms and look of the core**
    - Until the mid-20th century, custom set heights and street widths.
    - The article praises the **uniform colour of the house fronts around the Plaça de l'Església**.
    - Earlier written rules include municipal bylaws of 1872 and 1883, building norms of 1959, a plan of 1960 and subsidiary norms of 1992.
12. **Rivers and ravines:** the village sits between rieres and barrancs, and builders avoided flood-prone land. The **Barranc del Portal was culverted in 1980**, and the **Rieró was diverted in 1996**.

### Items the article does *not* give
- No precise extent (area or perimeter) of the walled core.
- No date for the walls.
- No plan of the castle.
- A separate press report adds these facts. They come from a search snippet; the page itself could not be retrieved.
  - Perimeter above **900 m** (historians Eugeni Perea and Jordi Morelló).
  - Walls started in the context of **1374**, when Pere III ordered towns to fortify.
  - First archaeologically documented section, found on **Carrer de Sant Jaume** in 2023: 8.25 m long and 3.42 m high.
  - Source: https://tarragonadigital.com/noticia/14444/descoberta-muralla-medieval-nucli-antic-riudoms
- ca.wikipedia (Riudoms) says Pere el Cerimoniós had walls and towers built at the end of the 14th century, and that none survive.
